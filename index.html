How it works

Before an IEP or 504 meeting, parents receive a stack of documents: the plan, evaluations, progress reports, sometimes a behavior plan. The meeting itself is short, and the school team does this every week. Many parents read the documents the night before and still do not know what to ask. Advocates offer written document reviews for a fee per review. IEP Prep gives you the same kind of written review at a price that fits a family budget.

You upload the documents you have: the current IEP or 504 plan, the most recent evaluations, progress reports and any behavior plan. You can add a few sentences about your concerns — what is not working at school, what your child tells you, what the teacher said.

The review reads the documents together. It looks at whether each goal has a starting point and a way to measure progress, whether services are described with how often and how long they happen, whether the needs described in the evaluations show up in the plan, and whether progress reports show movement toward the goals.

You receive a written review in plain language: a list of concerns, each pointing to the place in the document where it comes from; questions to ask at the meeting; and next steps — what to ask for, what to request in writing, what to follow up on afterwards.

The review is meant to help you take part in the meeting as an equal member of the team. It does not tell you what the school must do, and it does not replace an advocate or an attorney when there is a dispute.

Documents are stored in an encrypted account and can be deleted by you at any time. Nobody at your child's school sees them unless you share the review yourself.

Families who have several meetings a year can choose a monthly plan with several reviews, for example for an annual review, a reevaluation and a meeting after a change of school.

IEP Prep is for parents and caregivers in the United States. Plans and evaluations from any state can be uploaded; the review uses the documents you provide.

Before the meeting you can print the questions or keep them on your phone, and after it you can upload the new plan to see what changed compared with the previous one.

IEP Prep is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. Decisions about your child's education are made by you and the school team.
